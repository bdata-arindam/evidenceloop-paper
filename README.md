# EvidenceLoop

Reading Buys Evidence, Not a Decision: The Cost of Verification and the Limits of Retrieval.
ACLing 2026, Procedia Computer Science.

## What the study measures

A system asked not to assert what it cannot support must decide, for each claim, whether the
evidence settles it, and that decision costs reading. This study makes the reading budget
explicit and prices two things against it separately: whether the evidence has been read at
all, and whether the claim is labelled correctly. They are usually treated as one quantity.
They are not one quantity.

On 19,998 human-written claims against an encyclopaedia snapshot, balanced across supported,
refuted and not settled, raising the budget from one sentence to a hundred moves evidence
recall from 0.5892 to 0.9863, or from 0.5390 to 0.9453 if a complete annotator set is
required rather than any one sentence. The same increase moves the share labelled correctly
from 0.4001 to 0.4281, against a chance baseline of 0.3333. The two quantities differ by a
factor of about fourteen.

An oracle arm bounds what better retrieval could buy: promoting every settleable claim's own
annotator sentences to the top of the retrieved list is worth 0.0253 at one sentence and
nothing measurable from ten sentences on. The gap is therefore not in finding evidence.

Splitting the held-out claims by whether the complete evidence set actually arrived locates
the small accuracy gain away from the evidence: claims whose set never arrives gain 0.0505
between one sentence and a hundred, more than the 0.0418 gained by claims whose set does.

## What the verifier is

It reads ten summary statistics of the retrieved sentences: top and mean score, the gap and
decay of the score list, maximum, mean and spread of token coverage, the count of sentences
covering at least half the claim's tokens, the claim's token count and the number of distinct
pages spanned. No feature represents the meaning of a sentence. That is the measurement
rather than an oversight: the reported accuracy is what a system that can locate evidence but
not read it achieves.

## Reproducing it

The retrieval pool is not shipped. It is 75 MB derived from a 1.71 GB public snapshot, and
`src/evidenceloop/build_store.py` rebuilds it, recording the digest of what it downloads. The
claim file digest is in `results/run_manifest.json` and was re-verified against the source
before the revision.

Order: `build_store.py`, then `run_study.py`, then `paired_stats.py`, then `seed_stability.py`.
`llm_arm.py` runs the language model arm and will not overwrite an existing run.

## Two things to know about the records

The language model arm does not reproduce bit for bit. Budgets 1 and 100 return identical
accuracies; budget 5 moved by three claims of 198 and budget 20 by two. The cause is
non-deterministic reduction order in 4-bit quantised decoding rather than a change of method.
The reported run holds the canonical filenames; the re-run sits beside it.

The per-claim prompts in `results/supplement/` are reconstructed rather than captured. The
prompt is a deterministic function of the claim text and its retrieved sentences, both
retained with digests, so each is regenerated by the function the run calls and was checked
against the run's own assembly loop: 792 prompts, none differing.

## Data provenance

FEVER claims and the encyclopaedia snapshot, both public, retrieved with their locations and
cryptographic hashes recorded in the run manifest.
