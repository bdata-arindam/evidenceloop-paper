Nothing in the paper moved except where an analysis required it. The reproduction re-derived
every published value in Sections 3 to 6 exactly, to four decimal places.

REVIEWER 1
================================================================================

R1.1  The verifier is feature-only; this should be acknowledged more prominently and the
      conclusions restricted accordingly.

ACCEPTED. The second half was the part we had not done. The paper already stated the
restriction, including in the abstract, as the measurement rather than a caveat. What it did
not do was bound its own conclusions by it. The conclusion now carries an explicit scope
sentence: every accuracy reported is the accuracy of a verifier that reads retrieval
statistics rather than sentences, so the conclusions bound what such a system achieves and
what better retrieval is worth to it. They do not bound what a system reading the
evidence as language would achieve.

R1.2  Paired confidence intervals or significance tests for same-claim comparisons.

ACCEPTED in full. The reviewer is exactly right that we had not done this. The submitted
paper reported a binomial standard error of about 0.0049 and said it treated each held-out
claim as an independent trial. Every comparison the paper actually makes is between two
conditions scored on the same 9,999 claims, so that was the wrong error to quote.

We re-ran the study retaining per-claim predictions and recomputed every comparison as a
paired one: a bootstrap over claims with 10,000 resamples, plus McNemar's exact test on the
discordant pairs. The reconciliation that the per-claim records reproduce the published
accuracies is in the supplementary material and agrees at every budget.

The effect on the paper is that the oracle result got stronger and more specific, not weaker:

  budget   oracle minus feasible   95% paired interval   McNemar p
       1          +0.0253          +0.0162 to +0.0344     <0.0001
       2          +0.0168          +0.0075 to +0.0261      0.0005
       5          +0.0130          +0.0045 to +0.0215      0.0031
      10          -0.0016          -0.0101 to +0.0070      0.7272
      20          -0.0032          -0.0115 to +0.0050      0.4553
      50          -0.0021          -0.0096 to +0.0054      0.6008
     100          +0.0019          -0.0056 to +0.0094      0.6328

The submitted paper said perfect retrieval was worth "under one accuracy point on average",
which is true but blunt. The paired analysis says something much sharper: it is worth 2.5
points when the budget is one sentence; from ten sentences on it is worth nothing that
can be distinguished from zero. The paper now says that. Table 2 carries the intervals.

The headline budget comparison is likewise now paired: raising the budget from 1 to 100
raises accuracy by 0.0280, 95% interval 0.0162 to 0.0396, McNemar p below 0.0001 on 3,640
discordant claims.

R1.3  Materials were unavailable at review and the exact prompts were not retained.

ACCEPTED. The materials are now supplied. The manifest did record the model, the quantisation, the
decoding rule, the token limit and the prompt shape; what it did not record was the literal
prompt text. The supplementary material now carries the template verbatim, printed by the
same function the run executes rather than transcribed by hand, so what a reader sees is
the executed prompt by construction.

The per-claim prompts are supplied too, all 792 of them, one per claim per budget. Each
prompt is assembled at run time from the claim text and the sentences the retriever
returned at that budget; both of those inputs were retained with their digests. The
prompts are therefore regenerated by the same function the run calls. We checked that
regeneration against the run's own assembly loop rather than asserting it: 792 prompts,
none differing. We describe them as reconstructed rather than captured, because that is
what they are; the digests of both inputs ship with them so a reviewer can repeat the
reconstruction.

The completions supplied are the re-run's. Reconstructing a prompt does not reconstruct the
answer; we would rather say so than imply the original run retained more than it did.

The artifact bundle is listed in the supplementary material and is anonymised.

R1.4  Report precision alongside recall.

ALREADY IN THE PAPER. We think the reviewer was reading Figure 4, whose caption mentioned
only recall. Table 3 is titled "Recall and precision by label against sentences read" and
carries three precision columns. The body already stated the comparison the reviewer is
asking for: not-settled precision trails supported's at every budget and refuted's at five of
seven, so recall alone does not make it the easiest decision. The conclusion repeats it.

We have fixed what caused the confusion rather than added an analysis: the Figure 4 caption
now says that recall is plotted and points to Table 3 for precision. That figure is
numbered 2 in the revision, because two figures moved to the supplementary material.

REVIEWER 2
================================================================================

R2.1  Evidence retrieval is not evidence comprehension.

AGREED. This is the paper's own thesis, so the reviewer is telling us we did not say it
clearly enough at the point where it matters. The oracle paragraph now draws the line
explicitly: the bound says what promoting annotator sentences is worth to a classifier
reading retrieval statistics, not what better retrieval would be worth to a system that read
the sentences as language. The conclusion carries the same restriction as a scope sentence.

R2.2  NOT ENOUGH INFO is a class label, not an abstention decision; the link to selective
      classification should be qualified.

ACCEPTED. This was a genuine conflation and we are grateful it was caught. The submitted
paper wrote that withholding a verdict when evidence is insufficient "is the reject option
Chow analysed", which is wrong in a way that matters: our system predicts a third class, it
does not withhold anything.

The related-work passage now separates the two. A classifier with a reject option withholds a
decision it was asked to make, trading coverage against risk. Our verifier never withholds:
not settled is one of three classes it must choose between, it is scored like any other label
and coverage is fixed at one. Not settled is a property the corpus asserts about a claim,
not an abstention the verifier elects. We state that nothing in the paper measures a
risk-coverage trade-off. The selective-classification citations are now framed as an
analogy rather than as an instance.

R2.3  Annotator-first promotion is an upper bound for this setup, not a universal retrieval
      oracle.

ACCEPTED. The paper said "no analyst could do this, so it is an upper bound", which bounds
the feasibility but not the generality. The oracle paragraph now ends by binding the result
twice over: it is a bound on this verifier and on this intervention. The paired analysis
makes the restriction sharper rather than vaguer, because it shows the oracle's value is
confined to budgets below ten.

R2.4  Some conclusions are stronger than the evidence supports; narrow them to the evaluated
      conditions.

ACCEPTED. We made a claim-strength pass over the abstract, the discussion and the conclusion.
The substantive changes are: the oracle claim is now stated per budget with intervals instead
of as an average; the language model comparison is stated as matched on 198 claims and
explicitly described as bounding rather than settling; and the conclusion restricts every
accuracy claim to verifiers that read retrieval statistics. Where the evidence did not allow
narrowing we left the claim alone rather than hedging it decoratively.

REVIEWER 3
================================================================================

R3.1  Recall counts whether at least one annotator sentence was retrieved, not whether the
      complete evidence set was.

ACCEPTED. This was the most substantive point of the fourteen. The reviewer is right that
the submitted paper did not say which definition it used and right that it used the weaker
one.

We should note in our own defence only that the distinction was understood in the paper: the
related-work section defines the FEVER score as label accuracy counted where at least one
complete set of annotator evidence has been retrieved. The coverage curve simply did not use
that definition. So this is an addition rather than a correction of an error.

We computed both. The complete-set curve is lower at every budget:

  sentences read    at least one sentence    a complete annotator set
               1          0.5892                     0.5390
               2          0.7221                     0.6669
               5          0.8489                     0.7895
              10          0.9089                     0.8521
              20          0.9496                     0.8977
              50          0.9761                     0.9302
             100          0.9863                     0.9453

The headline is weaker under the stricter definition and the paper now reports it that way,
in the abstract, in Table 1, in the results and in the conclusion. The qualitative finding is
unchanged and in one respect is strengthened: after reading a hundred sentences, about one
settleable claim in eighteen still has no complete annotator set retrieved, which the
at-least-one measure hid. A supplementary figure plots the two curves together.

The central comparison is unaffected in direction. Evidence recall rises by about 40 points
under either definition while accuracy rises by 2.8, so the fourteenfold divergence the paper
reports holds under both.

R3.2  The retrieval pool contains the pages cited by the evaluation claims, so retrieval is
      easier here than searching the full encyclopaedia.

ALREADY DISCLOSED. We would rather point to it than add to it. The paper states the pool
construction in numbers — 2,892 cited pages plus 119,599 sampled distractors, 122,491 pages
of 5,416,537 scanned — and then states the consequence: a smaller pool makes retrieval easier
than the full snapshot, so every cost reported here is a lower bound. The discussion repeats
it among the boundaries.

We considered building an independently constructed retrieval set. We decided against it and
want to be straightforward about why: it is a different study rather than a revision;
with the pool as the independent variable it would change what the paper measures. We would
rather report the limitation accurately than half-address it.

R3.3  The oracle's gain is specific to this classifier and setup.

ACCEPTED; this is the same fix as R2.3 and the oracle paragraph now carries it. The paired
analysis also replaces the single averaged number with a per-budget statement, which is
harder to over-read.

R3.4  Oracle accuracy is non-monotonic across budgets. Explain it and give intervals.

ACCEPTED. The intervals turned out to be the whole answer. The reviewer correctly spotted
that oracle accuracy falls from 0.4285 at two sentences to 0.4162 at fifty. Paired, every
consecutive difference within that arm has an interval containing zero except 50 to 100.
The whole one-to-a-hundred difference is +0.0046 with an interval of -0.0073 to +0.0163.

Rather than leave it there, we did what the question actually calls for: we held everything
fixed except the seed, which controls both the train/test split and the classifier, then
repeated the study over twenty seeds. Seed 0 is the reported split and reproduces both arms
exactly. The result separates the dip into two parts.

The part the reviewer points at is resampling variation. On the reported split the
annotator-first minimum sits at fifty sentences; across twenty seeds it moves to six
different budgets, landing at one sentence on nine of them and at fifty on three.

The part we did not anticipate is real; it changes the paper. The annotator-first arm
rises from one sentence to two on twenty seeds out of twenty, falling from two to five on
nineteen out of twenty. The arm a system can actually run does not do this; its two-to-five
step rises on fifteen of twenty. So the drop belongs to the intervention rather than to the
budget. A reading consistent with that contrast, which we do not test, is that once the
annotator sentences are placed first every further sentence is a non-gold one, leaving the
statistics the verifier reads diluted by material that cannot settle the claim.

The submitted paper did not discuss the non-monotonicity; our first answer to this point
declined to interpret it. The paper now says which part of it is noise and which part is not. The supplementary material carries the
seed-by-seed tables and a figure.

One consequence we should disclose rather than let a reviewer find. The reported split is a
favourable one for this arm. The gain at one sentence is +0.0253 here against a twenty-seed
mean of +0.0197, third highest of the twenty; the gain at five sentences is the largest of
all twenty. At ten and twenty sentences the reported split is at the other end: it shows a small
negative where the seed mean is slightly positive. The direction of the oracle result is
unchanged and the gain is positive on twenty seeds out of twenty at one sentence; the
sharper reading, that the gain is confined below five sentences and absent thereafter, is
not supported by resampling. The paper now states the reported-split values, which come from
the seed fixed before the run, together with that qualification. We have not re-baselined the
paper onto across-seed means: the seed was fixed in advance, so changing the headline after
seeing which seed was favourable would be the wrong correction. We think that is the honest answer to the question as
asked. The full budget-by-budget paired table for both arms is in the supplementary material.

R3.5  Coverage rises while accuracy sometimes falls. This needs investigation.

ACCEPTED. This produced the result we found most interesting in the revision. We agree it
needed investigating rather than asserting, so we did the obvious thing with the per-claim
records: split the held-out claims by whether a complete annotator evidence set had actually
been retrieved, then measure the paired accuracy change within each group.

Over the full range, from one sentence to a hundred:

  group                                       claims   accuracy change   95% interval
  complete evidence arrived in that range      2,772       +0.0418       +0.0198 to +0.0642
  complete evidence already there at 1         3,625       -0.0050       -0.0240 to +0.0141
  complete evidence never arrived              3,602       +0.0505       +0.0303 to +0.0711

The gain is not where the evidence is. Claims whose evidence never arrives improve by more
than claims whose evidence does arrive; claims that already had their evidence at one
sentence do not improve at all. For this verifier, a larger budget changes the retrieval
statistics rather than supplying the decisive sentence, which is why coverage and accuracy
move independently.

This is now a paragraph in the paper and it reinforces its thesis by a second, independent
route: the oracle arm says better retrieval does not help; this split says the help a
larger budget does give is not coming from retrieval either. The full budget-by-budget table
is in the supplementary material.

R3.6  The language model is scored on 198 claims and the classifier on 9,999.

ACCEPTED. The matched comparison changed our claim, so we have weakened it. The subsample
is drawn deterministically from the same held-out half, stratified by label, so we reproduced
it exactly and rescored the feature verifier on precisely those 198 claims.

  sentences read   language model   feature verifier   difference   95% interval      McNemar p
               1       0.4697            0.3788         +0.0909   +0.0000 to +0.1818    0.0662
               5       0.4697            0.3990         +0.0707   -0.0253 to +0.1616    0.1654
              20       0.3990            0.4091         -0.0101   -0.1010 to +0.0808    0.9161

Paired on the same claims, none of the four differences is separable from zero. The submitted
paper said the language model "leads by 2.0 and 2.2 times that error at the two smaller
budgets", which overstated what 198 claims can show. The revised text reports the matched
numbers, states that no single comparison is settled and keeps only the observation that the
direction is consistent across budgets. The arm bounds the question rather than answering it.

One disclosure. The matched test needs the language model's per-claim predictions, which the
original run did not retain, so that arm was re-run. It reproduced one and a hundred sentences
exactly and moved by three claims at five and two at twenty, which is ordinary non-determinism
in 4-bit quantised decoding rather than a change of method. Both runs are tabulated in the
supplementary material and the paper reports the original run's values, which are the ones
submitted. We would rather state this than present the arm as exactly reproducible.

WHAT THIS REVISION LEAVES FOR FURTHER WORK
================================================================================

The analyses added here close some questions by opening others. We list them because
several are more interesting than what the paper settles, not to promise them.

  AN INDEPENDENTLY CONSTRUCTED RETRIEVAL POOL. Reviewer 3 asks for this and we decline it
  as a separate study rather than a revision. It is the right next experiment. The pool
  here is every cited page plus sampled distractors, which makes retrieval easier so that
  every cost is a lower bound. Rebuilding it without reference to the evaluation claims would
  turn that bound into a measurement.

  A VERIFIER THAT READS THE SENTENCES. Every accuracy here belongs to a system reading
  retrieval statistics rather than language, which the conclusion now says explicitly. The
  natural successor is the same budget sweep with a reader that understands the retrieved
  text, which would say whether the gap this paper measures is a property of the task or of
  this verifier.

  THE DILUTION READING, TESTED. The annotator-first arm falls from two sentences to five on
  nineteen of twenty seeds while the feasible arm rises. We offer dilution as a reading
  consistent with the contrast and we do not test it. A direct test would hold the gold
  sentences fixed and vary only the non-gold material placed after them.

  THE LANGUAGE MODEL ARM AT FULL SCALE. It runs on 198 claims at four of the seven budgets,
  which is why the matched comparison settles nothing. The full held-out half at all seven
  budgets would decide whether reading the sentences beats reading their statistics.

  MORE THAN TWENTY SEEDS, AND NESTED RESAMPLING. Twenty is enough to show the dip moves and
  to place the reported split in a distribution; it is a small sample for the percentile
  statements, which is why they are phrased as counts out of twenty rather than as
  population quantiles.

  COMPLETE-SET RECALL AS AN OPERATING TARGET. After a hundred sentences about one settleable
  claim in eighteen still has no complete annotator set retrieved. What it would cost to
  close that remainder, in sentences read, is not measured here.
